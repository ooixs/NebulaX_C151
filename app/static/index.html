GET STARTED

Check your first recording.

1. Choose a system and add files

Choose the train system you want to check, then add its original sensor files. Keep the filenames unchanged. For Doors, use one continuous recording. For other systems, you can add several files, up to 100 files, with a limit of 120 MB for each file. Files are checked one at a time.

2. Check the files and review the results

Select Check file or Check files. Results appear after the entire batch completes. Select a sequence, car or recording to inspect measurements. Use these findings to help plan an inspection. An engineer must still decide whether the train is fit for service.

3. Download your results

Select Download results (CSV) to save a table you can open in a spreadsheet. Select Download all check results, then choose tasks. Each includes the latest result for each filename; tasks with multiple recordings get their own folder in the ZIP.

Understanding the measurements

Metric comparisons use the uploaded dataset’s mean ±2 standard deviations (at least three valid items). Deviation is 100 × (selected value − mean) ÷ standard deviation: +150% SD means 1.5 SD above the mean. By absolute deviation, green fades to white at 50% SD; 50–100% is neutral; above 100% fades to dark red at 200% SD. A dataset with no variation shows 0% at the mean; insufficient data shows a dash. Trace outliers lie outside that trace’s mean ±3 standard deviations. These bands describe the data; they are not approved operating limits or explanations of the model’s decision. The comparison includes the selected item.

Door comparisons group opening and closing sequences separately. Air-conditioning compares cars in the same recording; green means lower inspection priority, not a confirmed healthy car. Rail readings depend on speed and sensor position. Structural comparisons require matching measurement point, duration, line and load condition. A high fatigue estimate is not a failure diagnosis.

Plots show a sample of the original readings plus the most extreme points; sample numbers refer to the selected sequence, car time series or recording. Missing/invalid readings remain unavailable. Old saved checks need to be rerun to gain measurement details.
